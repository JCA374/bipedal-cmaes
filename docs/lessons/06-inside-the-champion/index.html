Inside the champion

The trained walker is 932 numbers. What do they actually do? This lesson opens the network up: which hidden neurons fire when, which ones it can't walk without, and which senses it really uses.

After this lesson you can
  • read an activation heatmap and connect it to the walker's gait;
  • explain ablation (knock a neuron out, measure the damage) and what it tells you;
  • say which inputs this walker relies on, and what that means for its weaknesses.

1Watch the neurons while it walks

Below, the champion walks a typical track (the median of nine). Under it, every row is one of the 32 hidden neurons and every column one moment in time: blue = positive, orange = negative. The strip at the bottom shows when each foot is on the ground.

Look for vertical stripes that repeat: neurons switching in step with the feet. A walker without memory still produces a rhythm, because each leg's position feeds back as input. The rhythm lives in the loop between body and network, not in the network alone.

Check yourself

1. Freezing hidden neuron 7 drops the score by 2 points; freezing neuron 12 drops it by 150. What's the best conclusion?

Ablation measures dependence, not weight size. A big weight on an input that never changes can matter very little.

2. The network has no memory. Where does its rhythm come from?

Body and network form a loop: the action moves the legs, and the new leg state changes the next action.

3. If freezing the lidar barely changes the score, what follows?

The normal tracks are gentle enough to walk by feel. That's a hypothesis to check against the falls in lesson 8.
