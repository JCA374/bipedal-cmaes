The pattern: gradient methods (PPO, TD3) learn from individual steps and need far fewer simulation steps. Evolution only sees whole-episode totals, so it needs many more steps, but it's simple, parallel and very stable, and here it finds a policy about 140 times smaller than TD3's actor.

5The full analysis

Two reports go deeper, following the same method: record every step of 200–300 episodes on separate analysis tracks, compute candidate signals, and ask one question per figure.

  • Score analysis: where the points go, who finishes in time, what predicts a low score, and how much effort is wasted on joints that aren't moving.
  • Fall analysis: what happens before the rare falls.

Check yourself

1. The walker reaches the finish on most tracks, but averages well under 300. Why?

The finish is worth about 360 in progress. Whatever is missing went to effort (and the occasional fall or time-out).

2. Why compare with NEAT on the same 100 tracks instead of using each project's own numbers?

Track difficulty affects both walkers the same way and cancels out of the difference (lesson 4).

3. TD3 scores higher with far fewer environment steps. What does CMA-ES offer instead?

ES trades samples for simplicity: no critic, no replay buffer, no learning-rate schedules, and a 932-number policy.
