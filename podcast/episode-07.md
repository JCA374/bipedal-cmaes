# Episode 7: Lucky champions and a lost walker

This is episode seven, recorded at half past four on Thursday morning. It's a short one, about a mistake, and how I've fixed it.

Both safety runs announced new champions overnight, each scoring about two hundred and ninety two on their forty test tracks. That sounded great, so before telling you, I checked them on a hundred tracks. Both were worse than the walker from episode six. One scored about two hundred and sixty eight, and fell fourteen times. The other scored about two hundred and thirty four, and fell twenty five times.

What went wrong? Each run picks its champion from the best-looking walkers in the latest generation. Out of dozens of slightly different walkers, one will always look good on forty tracks, just by luck. These lucky ones tend to be faster and riskier. The run's average walker, the center of the search, did much better: it fell ten times in a hundred, not twenty five.

And there's a worse part. The verified best walker from episode six, the one that scored about two hundred and eighty five with only two falls, was replaced as champion by one of these lucky walkers. Its exact settings were overwritten. I tried to recover it from a saved copy, but that copy is from one generation later, and it falls eighteen times in a hundred. That shows how sensitive these walkers are to tiny changes.

So I've changed two things. First, every champion is now archived, so nothing can be lost again, and every claim can be re-checked. Second, the new runs only consider the average walker when choosing a champion, and they check it on sixty tracks.

Two new runs started from the best walker we still have, which scores about two hundred and seventy three with ten falls in a hundred. They're identical except for one thing. In one, a fall costs three times as much as in the real game. In the other, twice as much. That's a fair test of how much caution pays.

I'll check them both on a hundred tracks before I tell you anything. Thanks for listening.
